Research Lab

Everything a jury asks for: the math model, QPSO vs exact / PSO / GA / ACO / SA with statistics, shortest path on the road graph, and live re-planning when traffic changes.

1. Mathematical formulation (CVRPTW on a weighted graph)

G = (V, E, w)     V = {0 (depot), 1..n customers},  E = road segments
t_ij = t0_ij · (1 + 0.15 · (v/c)^4) + δ_ij      BPR congestion + incident delay
minimise  F = Σ d_ij·x_ij + 0.4·Σ T_k + λ_cap·Σ max(0, L_k − Q) + λ_tw·Σ max(0, a_i − due_i) + λ_fleet·max(0, K_used − K)
subject to  each customer visited exactly once;  L_k = Σ demand_i ≤ Q (capacity);  a_i ≤ due_i (time window);  ≤ K vehicles
QPSO (quantum-inspired, classical):  p = φ·pbest + (1−φ)·gbest;   x ← p ± α·|mbest − x|·ln(1/u),   α: 0.95 → 0.5   (Sun et al. 2004)
      + Lamarckian 2-opt / or-opt local search and heuristic seeding
Constraints are handled by fixed penalty weights inside F; solutions are decoded from random keys.

2. Statistical benchmark vs exact optimum

Repeated independent runs. Optimality gap is measured against exhaustive search (exact) on the same instance.

Press “Run benchmark”. Exact solver runs only up to 9 customers (n! growth) — beyond that gaps are relative to the best found.

3. Convergence analysis

Mean best-cost per iteration across runs (lower is better).

4. Shortest path on a congested road graph

8×8 city grid, BPR travel times. Dijkstra gives the exact answer; QPSO is checked against it.

5. Dynamic re-optimisation

Plan under normal traffic, then a congestion shock hits. Keep the old plan, or re-plan with QPSO?

Uses 15 random delivery stops with time windows.
